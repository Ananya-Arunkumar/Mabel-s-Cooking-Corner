September 21st, 2014 - Grandma's Apple Pie!

Return to home

Hey everyone!

With autumn just around the corner, I thought it would be the perfect time to share one of my very favorite recipes: my grandma's apple pie! Grandma used to make this whenever the weather started getting chilly, and the whole house would smell like cinnamon and baked apples. I remember sneaking little bits of the filling when she wasn't looking (although I don't think I was nearly as sneaky as I imagined!). I've made a few little adjustments over the years, but this recipe still reminds me of those afternoons in her kitchen. I hope you enjoy it as much as I do! Make sure you let the pie cool before cutting it. I know it's difficult to wait when it smells so good, but trust me, it's worth it!

Happy baking, everyone!

- Mabel ♡

Prep: 30 mins

Bake: 50-60 mins

Serves: 8

For the Crust:

  • 2½ cups (315 g) all-purpose flour
  • 1 cup (227 g) cold unsalted butter, cubed
  • ½ teaspoon salt
  • 6–8 tablespoons ice water

For the Filling:

  • 6 medium apples, peeled, cored, and thinly sliced
  • ¾ cup (150 g) granulated sugar
  • 1 teaspoon ground cinnamon
  • ¼ teaspoon ground nutmeg (optional)
  • 1 tablespoon lemon juice
  • 2 tablespoons all-purpose flour or cornstarch
  • 1 tablespoon butter, cut into small pieces

For the Top:

  • 1 egg, beaten (optional, for an egg wash)
  • 1 teaspoon sugar, for sprinkling (optional)

Instructions:

  1. Mix the flour and salt in a bowl. Cut in the cold butter until the mixture resembles coarse crumbs. Add ice water a tablespoon at a time until the dough just comes together. Divide into two discs, wrap, and chill for at least 30 minutes.

  2. In a large bowl, toss the sliced apples with the sugar, cinnamon, nutmeg, lemon juice, and flour or cornstarch

  3. Preheat the oven to 400°F (200°C). Roll out one disc of dough and fit it into a 9-inch pie plate. Trim the excess pastry.

  4. Spoon the apple mixture into the crust and dot the filling with the tablespoon of butter.

  5. Roll out the second disc and place it over the filling. Trim and crimp the edges to seal. Cut a few slits in the top to let steam escape. Brush with beaten egg and sprinkle with sugar if desired.

  6. Bake at 400°F (200°C) for 20 minutes, then reduce the oven temperature to 375°F (190°C) and bake for another 30–40 minutes, until the crust is golden and the filling is bubbling. Cover the edges with foil if they brown too quickly.

  7. Leave the pie to cool for at least 2–3 hours before slicing so the filling has time to set.

I always think of Grandma when I make this pie. She taught me that the best recipes are the ones you share with the people you love. I made one for Terrence last week, too! He sat by the oven the whole time waiting for a piece.
